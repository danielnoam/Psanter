# How a major scale is built

A major scale is eight notes with the same pattern of steps from any key:
whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half. A half step is the very next
key; a whole step skips one. In C that's all white keys. Start on G and the
pattern needs F♯.

The fingering is the same as C major: thumb under after 3, then after 4.

```abc practice: tempo=60 hands=right loop=true
X:1
T:G major, right hand
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:G
!1!G !2!A !3!B !1!c !2!d !3!e !4!f !5!g | !4!f !3!e !2!d !1!c !3!B !2!A !1!G2 |]
```

```abc practice: tempo=60 hands=left loop=true
X:2
T:G major, left hand
M:4/4
L:1/8
Q:1/4=60
K:G clef=bass
!5!G,, !4!A,, !3!B,, !2!C, !1!D, !3!E, !2!F, !1!G, | !2!F, !3!E, !1!D, !2!C, !3!B,, !4!A,, !5!G,,2 |]
```
