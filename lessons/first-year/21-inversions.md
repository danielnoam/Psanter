# Inversions

The same three notes can be stacked in another order. With the root at the
bottom it's **root position**; move the root up an octave and the third is
at the bottom (**first inversion**); move the third up too and the fifth is
at the bottom (**second inversion**). It's still the same chord.

```abc practice: tempo=60 hands=right
X:1
T:C major, up through its inversions
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
[CEG]2 [EGc]2 | [Gce]2 [ceg]2 | [Gce]2 [EGc]2 | [CEG]4 |]
```

Inversions let the hand stay put while the chords change. C, F and G
without moving much:

```abc practice: tempo=60 hands=right
X:2
T:Close together
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=60
K:C
[CEG]4 | [CFA]4 | [B,DG]4 | [CEG]4 |]
```
